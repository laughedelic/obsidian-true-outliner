# Adding a node with the pointer: what the surfaces are made of

**Measured 17 September 2026** against `main` at `8b9335b`, at the pure level — `parse`,
`computeLineGuides` and `ancestryAtLine` over the test vault (29 notes, 698 lines, 367 nodes), plus
`arbMarkdownText` (3000 documents) and `arbTree` (600 documents) from `tests/generators.ts`,
fast-check seed 42. The script is
[`prototypes/insert-affordance/guide-ends.ts`](prototypes/insert-affordance/guide-ends.ts); the
interactive comparison of the candidate surfaces is
[insert-affordance-mockup.html](insert-affordance-mockup.html), generated by
[`prototypes/insert-affordance/mockup.mjs`](prototypes/insert-affordance/mockup.mjs).

**Recorded, not proposed.** Nothing here is chosen. The measurement exists so the surface question
is settled on figures, and the mockup exists so the treatments are tried before one is picked.

## The report

Planting a node under a subtree means placing the caret at the end of that subtree's last line and
pressing Enter. Two things make it finicky. The caret placement is a small target reached by eye,
and missing it costs a keyboard walk to the line's end. And Enter alone is usually not the whole
keypress: it opens a sibling of the line the caret is on, at *that* line's depth, so every level
between that line and the intended parent is an outdent afterwards.

What is wanted is a pointer-driven control — a `+` at the end of a guide line to begin with, and
possibly mid-tree where the structure allows one. Adding a plain child or sibling to a node that has
no subtree at all is in scope too, which the coverage figure below turns from a possibility into a
requirement.

## What already occupies these surfaces

- **A guide has no element.** Experiment 2b draws every guide as `repeating-linear-gradient` layers
  on one `::after` per line ([experiment-2-guide-lines.md](experiment-2-guide-lines.md)), so no
  guide can be hovered or clicked as a DOM node. Both gestures are arithmetic against the painted
  background: `guideHit` in `src/plugin/zoom-click.ts` for the hit test, and the hover itself is
  editor state rather than a style, so it survives every decoration rebuild
  (`src/plugin/guide-hover.ts`).
- **A guide already carries a press.** It folds or unfolds every child of its owner
  (`toggleGuideAt`), within a band reaching `unit/2 - 2` left of the column and `unit/3` right of
  it — wider on the left because the node one level in owns the run to the right
  ([fold-mechanics.md](fold-mechanics.md)).
- **A guide already lights up under the pointer,** at `--to-guide-hover-width`, on every line it
  runs through.
- **The slot half a unit left of a marker is the fold chevron's,** at
  `--to-fold-chevron-offset`, floored so a narrow unit does not bring it in over a checkbox.
- **A marker press zooms** (`src/plugin/zoom-click.ts`).
- **The area past a line's own text is the folded-node placeholder's,** which already draws the
  hidden-descendant count and the way back there.
- **A node that is not there yet is an existing concept.** A provisional position is an empty place
  standing for a node, rendered as the node it would become, and removed when abandoned
  (`src/plugin/provisional-cleanup.ts`, `outline-decorations`,
  [provisional-position-guides.md](provisional-position-guides.md)). Any control considered here can
  land on one rather than writing text.

## How the figures were taken

A guide's **foot** is the place a control would go: the last line a guide runs through, paired with
the column it is drawn at. The measurement walks `computeLineGuides`' facts and records a foot
wherever a line carries a column that the line below does not, on either of the two tracks
(`guideDepths` and `listGuideDepths` both render). Each foot's owner is `ancestryAtLine(doc,
line)[column]`, the same resolution the fold gesture uses.

Two derived quantities carry most of the reading. **Clearance** is the foot's host line's tree depth
minus the guide's own column — how far right of the foot that line's own content starts, in levels.
**What sits below** is the line after the host: a blank line, a content line, or the end of the note.

## Findings

### F1 — One foot per parent, exactly

| | test vault | `arbMarkdownText` | `arbTree` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nodes | 367 | 7528 | 36,087 |
| Parents | 155 (42.2%) | 1164 (15.5%) | 16,013 (44.4%) |
| Leaves | 212 (57.8%) | 6364 (84.5%) | 20,074 (55.6%) |
| Guide feet | **155** | **1164** | **16,013** |
| Feet per 100 lines | 22.2 | 7.8 | 21.9 |

The identity holds on every corpus: a node owning a guide ends it once, and a node owning none ends
none. So a control hung off a foot reaches parents and nothing else — **57.8% of the test vault's
nodes are leaves**, and a leaf has no guide for such a control to appear on. A guide-only affordance
cannot plant a first child anywhere.

### F2 — A foot never lands on a blank line

Zero of the 17,332 feet across the three corpora sit on a blank gap line. `trimGapTails` narrows
every gap line's depths to the intersection with the content line below it, so a column a gap line
carries is a column the next line carries too, and a guide can only stop on content. The host is
always a real line with a real node on it.

### F3 — The host line's gutter is always free

Clearance never falls below 1 on any corpus. Distribution in the test vault: 1 → 91 (58.7%),
2 → 35, 3 → 16, 4 → 8, 5 → 3, 6 → 1, 7 → 1. A foot therefore sits at least one whole unit left of
its host line's own content start — inside that line's gutter, where the guide is already painted
and no marker, chevron or glyph of that line can be. The foot's own line can host a control at every
foot in every corpus.

### F4 — The line below a foot is never free

Where the line below is content, its clearance against the foot's column is at most 0 — it is 0 in
21.9% of the vault's feet (the next line's own marker sits exactly on the foot's column) and
negative in 5.1% (its text is already left of it). Nothing below a foot can host a control at the
foot's column. What sits there:

| | test vault | `arbMarkdownText` | `arbTree` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a blank line | 68.4% | 5.8% | 83.7% |
| a shallower node's content | 27.1% | 38.6% | 14.7% |
| the end of the note | 4.5% | 55.7% | 1.6% |

So "draw the control just below where the guide stops" is not available as stated. The nearest thing
that is, at every foot, is the bottom of the host line's own gutter.

### F5 — Feet crowd, and the crowding is horizontal

Feet per line in the test vault: 1 → 56 (61.5%), 2 → 19, 3 → 8, 4 → 5, 5 → 2, 7 → 1. **38.5% of
lines that end any guide end two or more.** The worst in the vault is
`test-vault/Backlinks/Family tree.md` line 37, which closes seven at columns 0 through 6;
`Backlinks/Branching arms.md` line 12 closes five.

Because a foot's column is a tree depth, co-terminating feet land on one line at *distinct columns,
one unit apart* — a horizontal run left of the text, not a vertical pile. Drawn unconditionally that
is a row of seven controls; read the other way, the run is the set of depths a new node could take
at that spot, laid out on the grid that already means depth.

### F6 — The keyboard route's cost is the clearance

Enter at the end of a subtree's last line opens a sibling of that line. Reaching the intended parent
from there is `clearance - 1` outdents, so F3's distribution is also the distribution of the
keypress cost: **41.3% of the vault's feet need at least one outdent after Enter**, and the deepest
needs six. On `arbTree` the same figure is 41.8% of 16,013 feet; on `arbMarkdownText`, whose trees
are shallow, only 6.8%.

The same number bounds the ambiguity of any single control at a foot: a foot with clearance *k*
stands for *k* different depths a new node could take, from a last child of the guide's owner out to
a sibling of the deepest last descendant. Only the 58.7% with clearance 1 are unambiguous.

## What a control can mean

Three intents are distinct, and no one surface expresses all three:

- **A last child of N**, appended below N's whole subtree. This is the foot, and it is the intent the
  report names. Available on parents only.
- **A first child of N**, directly below N's own lines. The only intent that reaches a leaf, and the
  one a foot cannot express at all.
- **A sibling after N**, below N's subtree at N's own depth. At a foot this is the same *place* as a
  last child of N's parent, at a different column.

The first and third differ only by column, which is why the depth run of F5 is a genuine candidate
rather than only a crowding problem.

## The candidate surfaces

All six are live in [insert-affordance-mockup.html](insert-affordance-mockup.html), drawn at the
plugin's own geometry over two real vault notes.

| | Surface | Reaches | The cost it carries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T1** | A control at every foot, always | parents | The control, not a candidate: 22 per 100 lines and up to seven on one line |
| **T2** | Guide hover reveals one control at that guide's foot | parents | Reuses `guideHoverField` and the existing band; adds a second meaning to a hover that already means "press to fold" |
| **T3** | Row hover reveals a control in the node's chevron slot | every node | Collides with the fold chevron on exactly the nodes that have one |
| **T4** | Row hover reveals a control past the node's own text | every node | Competes with placing the caret there, and with the folded-node placeholder |
| **T5** | Hovering a foot's band reveals one control per candidate depth | parents | Answers F6's ambiguity directly; costs a second gesture and a horizontal aim |
| **T6** | The same zones, each showing a ghost node row at that depth | parents | Says what will happen rather than offering a button, and the rendering already exists |

The two families are not rivals. A foot answers "append to this subtree", where the depth is
ambiguous and a column is the natural way to pick it. A node-hung control answers "give this node a
child", which is unambiguous and is the only route to the 57.8% of nodes that are leaves. A design
that ships one without the other leaves an intent unreachable.

## What the measurement settles

- The host is the foot's own line, in its gutter — never the line below it (F3, F4).
- A foot's column is a depth, so co-terminating feet are a depth run and not noise (F5).
- A guide-hung control cannot be the whole answer, whatever its treatment (F1).
- Nothing needs to be inserted to open a place: a provisional position is already a node that is not
  there yet, and abandoning one already removes it.

## What the drag change already carries

`node-dragging` (`feat/drag-nodes-with-a-drop-preview`, read at `ae06d0e`) resolves the same object
from the other end — a pointer choosing a seam and a column with a run in hand — and has already
built and measured what an insertion needs. Verified against that branch's source, not its
description:

- **The seam resolver takes no run.** `src/drop-destinations.ts` splits into `seams(doc, { folded,
  scoped, outside?, kind? })`, which finds every seam and the places it offers — parent, index,
  depth — from the tree alone, and `dropSeams(...)`, which adds what a *run* becomes at each place.
  `kind` defaults to `'paragraph'` and is what an insertion passes. Two rules live in `seams` and
  apply to an insertion exactly as to a drop: a non-heading node is not offered a depth at which it
  would follow a heading as its sibling (`followsAHeading` — a paragraph written after a heading is
  inside it whatever column was aimed at), and under a zoom the root column is not offered. F4 and
  the drag's end-seam fix are the same finding: the last seam anchors on the last line with text,
  not below it.
- **The preview is a decoration.** `src/plugin/drag-preview.ts` draws the seam layer and the ghost
  mark from `dragPreviewField`, and `decorations.ts` reads them in the same pass as the guides. T6's
  ghost row is that preview with an empty payload: the same column arithmetic in the overlay's own
  coordinate space, the same `GhostMarkWidget` with the kind stated rather than re-encoded. One
  thing not to rediscover: the ghost is a marker icon, and `MarginCompensation` writes
  `left … !important` inline onto every marker icon it finds on a plain line, which put the drag's
  ghost three columns out until it was excluded (`node-drag-and-drop` 6d). A ghost row needs the
  same exclusion.
- **An absorbed region is already settled.** A heading dropped at a seam takes the anchor's
  following siblings; `DropDestination.absorbs` carries the span, and the preview draws those rows
  one level in under the ghost, untinted. A heading inserted at a foot absorbs the same way and can
  read it from the same place.
- **The unit resolves once,** in `src/plugin/drag-geometry.ts`'s probe element, because
  `getComputedStyle` returns the unresolved `2rem` for the custom property. A foot control drawn
  from state reads it there.

So the open question "what a press dispatches" has a narrower shape than it did: the place comes
from `seams`, the drawing from the preview field, and only the act — open a provisional position at
that place, or insert — is this change's own.

## What it does not settle

- **Which treatment.** T2 is the cheapest against the existing machinery; T5 and T6 are the only
  ones that address F6's ambiguity; T6 needs no new iconography.
- **Whether a node-hung surface ships with it,** and if so which of T3 or T4 — the chevron collision
  against the caret-placement collision.
- **Touch.** None of the six exists without a pointer. Every treatment here needs a touch answer of
  its own or an explicit decision to be desktop-only, which the mode indicators already had to take
  ([outline-mode-surfaces.md](outline-mode-surfaces.md)).
- **What a press dispatches** — a provisional position at the chosen depth, or a real insertion.
  The first reuses `provisional-cleanup` and the caret policy wholesale; the second needs its own
  operation and its own history entry. Either way the place is `seams`' and the drawing is the
  preview field's (above).
- **Whether any of it is a setting,** and what the default is for a reader who wants no controls in
  the text at all.
